# Qwen Findings & Improvement Plan

Based on three full eval runs (47 weighted questions, 8 capabilities) against the Shanghai Dessert Guide agent after migrating it from Claude to Qwen (`qwen-plus` for chat, `qwen-vl-max` for vision).

## What the eval revealed about Qwen's ability

### 1. Grounding under open-ended generation is weaker than expected
When a question has a single clear fact to look up, Qwen grounds it reliably (Factual lookup: 1.86/2). But for open-ended "recommend something" questions — no single fact to retrieve, just a judgment call — it sometimes skips calling `retrieve_info` entirely and invents a plausible-sounding shop instead. In the worst case caught, it explicitly said *"I checked your history and it's empty... the knowledge base didn't return any entries"* — correctly reporting it found nothing — **then invented a fake shop anyway.** This persisted across repeated sampling of the identical prompt (roughly 1-in-3 to 1-in-4), and lowering temperature to 0.2 did not fix it. This is a rule-adherence gap, not a randomness artifact.

### 2. "Stretching" a real fact to cover a gap
When no knowledge-base entry actually matches a specific request (e.g. a French macaron specialist that doesn't exist in the data), Qwen doesn't reliably say so. Instead it sometimes picks an unrelated real store and invents supporting "evidence" — in the case caught, a fabricated *"many customers say this tastes close to a professional macaron shop"* claim — to make the stretch sound credible. Reproduced identically across two separate runs. This is arguably worse than plain invention: it wraps a false claim around a real store's real, accurate details, which makes it more convincing rather than less.

### 3. Over-elaboration on low-stakes prompts
Chit-chat was the single worst-scoring capability (0.80/2) in every run. Brief, casual questions ("do you like dessert?") reliably get long, embellished replies — invented personal history, fabricated shop tips, and twice, fully invented pseudo-scientific citations (a fake "2026 study," fake "China Youth Daily" references) — when the expected behavior was a short, natural, in-persona remark.

### 4. Self-grading (LLM-as-judge) is a real, demonstrated weakness
Using `qwen-plus` to judge its own answers produced at least two confirmed cases where the judge asserted a real knowledge-base entry ("EAU Café") was "invented" or "non-existent" — a hallucination in the grading rationale itself, separate from whatever it was grading. This isn't a theoretical same-model-bias concern; it showed up directly in the data.

### 5. Occasional dropped tool calls
A minority of transit-routing questions didn't trigger `get_transit_directions` at all, non-deterministically — same question, different outcome across runs. Not yet root-caused the way the retrieval bug below was.

### 6. Minor output-level quirks
- Qwen defaults to curly/smart punctuation (’) rather than straight ASCII — cosmetic, but it broke naive string-matching checks that assumed a straight apostrophe.
- It occasionally names its own internal tool (`retrieve_info`) by name while explaining a refusal — a small architecture-detail leak, not a full prompt leak, but worth noting.

### 7. What it does well (for balance)
- **Multi-turn context retention: perfect (2.00/2)** across every phrasing tested.
- **Factual lookup: strong (1.86/2).**
- Once given complete information, it aggregates correctly: fixing a retrieval bug (not a Qwen problem — see below) took Synthesis from 1.07/2 to 1.50/2. The earlier failure was as much a retrieval/context-completeness problem as a model-reasoning one — worth separating "Qwen reasons badly" from "Qwen wasn't given what it needed" before blaming the model.

### 8. The vision path had the same false-confidence bug, just undiscovered until now
`identify_exhibit` (Qwen-VL, matches an uploaded photo against the knowledge base) had zero eval coverage — building a small vision eval slice with real photos immediately surfaced the exact same pattern as findings #1/#2, just on a different model and input type. Tested against 6 real photos (2 of the same brand from different angles, 2 unrelated real desserts, 2 storefronts not in the KB): it never actually said "unknown" unless there was zero topical overlap at all. A photo of an Azabuya matcha cup with **no address or branch marker visible** still got a single, specific, wrong branch id back — a *different* wrong branch each time re-tested, proving it was guessing, not reading a real signal. Worse: a photo of a **Basque cheesecake** (an unrelated dessert) was force-matched to the matcha-only gelato brand anyway, and a **banana cream pie** photo was matched to a real pie shop whose actual menu doesn't include that flavor at all — a real store with a fabricated specific claim, the vision-side version of finding #2's "stretching."

## Planned next steps

1. ~~**Recommendation/stretching hallucination**~~ **— done, verified.** Added a post-hoc check in `agent.py` (`_looks_like_unverified_shop_claim`): after a draft reply, flag it if it makes a concrete claim (price/address/distance, or a bolded/quoted proper noun) about a shop that isn't a real knowledge-base brand and isn't attributed to a web search/community note — then force exactly one corrective retry with an automated notice, rather than trusting the prompt alone. Verified with 8 fresh samples of the hardest no-signal case: 0 fabrications (was fabricating in nearly every sample before). Directly observed the correction firing live — a caught draft's corrected reply opened with *"you're right, I didn't follow the rules just now"* before giving a properly grounded answer.

   **Escalates finding #4 below**: a judge-graded batch run scored this fix at 0.40/2, apparently regressed — but the underlying replies showed the judge calling a real, repeatedly-verified brand ("EAU Café") "fabricated" while describing text that never even mentioned it. Direct verification against real data is currently more trustworthy than the judge score for this capability.

2. ~~**"Stretching" a real entry with fake support**~~ **— done, verified (three rounds of fixes).** First pass (extending the check with a testimonial regex) turned out to only match the literal phrasing of the one caught example — re-sampling the identical question live showed the model paraphrases endlessly (食客反馈/大众点评上不少笔记提到/很多顾客会联想...) and none of the paraphrases matched. Broadened the regex to the general shape (aggregate-opinion subject + hearsay verb, allowing a gap between them, plus a review-platform-name variant) instead of specific wording, carefully re-excluding the two sanctioned attribution phrases ("有访客提到"/"访客反馈") so legitimate community-note citations don't get flagged. Verified against all previously-caught real examples plus new ones found live: 9/9 correct (including a false-positive guard for the agent legitimately *offering* to check social platforms, which the first draft of the broadened regex briefly over-matched).

   A second, deeper gap surfaced in the same testing: naming a real brand isn't the same as the specific claim about it being true. Live sampling of `recommendation_long_01` caught the model recommending a real "Azabuya" branch while inventing a **"Matcha #3 gelato"** variant (the real data only documents #5 as intensely bitter — no #3 exists), and a `recommendation_multiintent_01` sample separately invented ¥35/¥40 prices for a real shop. The old check only verified the brand name was real, so a fabricated number attached to a real name sailed straight through. Added a cross-check: extract any price/distance/menu-variant-number token from the draft reply and verify it actually appears in this turn's own `retrieve_info`/tool results before letting it pass — not just checking that *a* real brand was named somewhere. Verified against both the reproduced fabrication (correctly flagged) and a legit reply using only real retrieved numbers (correctly not flagged).

   A third variant, caught on a vague "old-school Chinese dessert shop" question with no real KB match: the model answered with six real, famous, non-fictional Shanghai heritage brands (沈大成, 乔家栅, 杏花楼...) and specific founding years — **while explicitly prefacing the answer with "根据知识库" ("per the knowledge base")** — none of which exist anywhere in `knowledge/*.json`. No price/address/testimonial pattern fires on this kind of plain prose, so it was completely invisible to every prior check. Added a targeted check: whenever a reply explicitly claims KB-sourcing this directly, at least one thing it names must actually be a real entry, or it's flagged. This is now a dedicated eval case (`kb_boundary_ambiguous_01`).

   **Still open, found while chasing the above**: even when the model is honest that the KB has no match, it sometimes still volunteers real-world facts about real (non-KB) businesses without attributing them as general knowledge or a web search — not a fabricated shop, and not a false KB-attribution claim either, just an unattributed specific-sounding fact slipped in as a "helpful aside." Softer than the three patterns above and not yet checked for; needs its own detection approach (rule 3 already requires attribution for anything outside the KB — this is that rule being followed loosely rather than broken outright).

3. ~~**Chit-chat over-elaboration**~~ **— done, verified.** Added rule 9 (1-2 sentences for casual turns, no invented personal history, no invented supporting "facts" of any kind) and extended the same post-hoc verification from #1/#2 with a citation/biochemistry-term detector — this app's knowledge base has zero nutrition/psychology content, so any unattributed study/statistic claim is essentially always fabricated. Verified: re-ran the full 5-question capability (10 cases) — **0.80/2 → 1.80/2**. The one remaining "failure" looks like judge inconsistency, not a real issue (the identical claim passed in English, failed in Chinese, for the same question). Did not end up needing the `max_tokens` structural approach floated below — the rule + verification combination was enough.

4. **Judge reliability** — either accept the same-family limitation as a documented tradeoff (cheapest), or have the judge quote the specific fact it's disputing before scoring, forcing it to ground its own criticism rather than assert freely. Now backed by eight+ separate confirmed cases of the judge asserting something flatly false, not three: two surfaced in a routine re-run (a chit-chat reply's "I *love* helping people discover amazing ones" called "a fabricated preference" — it's in-persona enthusiasm, not a falsifiable claim — and "我...不吃甜品哦"/"I don't eat dessert" called "an invented personal fact" when that's literally true for an AI); three more surfaced verifying the "stretching" fixes below — the judge called `recommendation_short_01`'s "抹茶番石榴气疯" and `synthesis_short_01`'s "千目抹茶卷" "invented products" when both are real menu items copy-pasted straight out of `knowledge/eau_cafe_henglong.json` and `knowledge/bebaked_yuyuan.json`, and called `recommendation_ambiguous_01`'s reply "fabricated addresses and dishes" when every distance (490m, 400m) and menu item in it matched the real KB entries exactly. Every one of these looked like a real regression in the raw score until the underlying reply text and the actual KB files were read side by side — that verification step is no longer optional, it's load-bearing. A ninth surfaced verifying the Bao's Pastry fix (item 6): a reply that explicitly said "两个不同品牌" (two different brands), correctly flagged Bao's Pastry as not in the KB, and offered to web-search it, still scored 0/2 grounded — because it led with Drunk Baker's real details before circling back to that disclaimer, and the judge read the ordering as fabrication rather than the actual content.

5. **Dropped tool calls** — not yet root-caused. Next step is comparing tool-call presence across repeated identical calls to see if it correlates with phrasing/language before deciding on a fix, the same way the retrieval bug was isolated.

6. ~~**Vision-path false confidence**~~ **— done, verified.** `identify_exhibit` originally just asked Qwen-VL "which id, or unknown" and trusted the bare answer. Rebuilt it to ask for structured evidence instead (a specific dish/item visible, and any address/sign text visible) and verify that evidence before trusting the match, rather than trusting a bare id:
   - **Food claim vs. real menu**: a second, cheap text-only `qwen-plus` call checks whether the claimed dish is actually plausible for that specific entry's real menu (ignoring plating/presentation) — catches the cheesecake→gelato-brand and banana-pie→wrong-flavor cases outright, returning `unknown` instead of a wrong match. Plain keyword overlap wasn't enough here: it turned out too weak — generic words like "pie" and "cream" trivially overlap with a pie shop's menu even when the specific flavor isn't on it, so a real verification call (analogous to how the text side's `judge()` works) was needed instead.
   - **Location claim vs. real address**: when the model can't point to a legible address/sign that matches the specific candidate branch's own address, and sibling branches of the same brand exist (same brand, multiple locations — Azabuya, bebaked, EAU Café, Pie Bird all have this shape), it now returns an `"ambiguous"` result listing the real candidate branches instead of guessing one — the same pattern `get_transit_directions` already uses for an ambiguous place name (`ambiguous: true` + `options`).
   - Re-tested `identify_exhibit` alone against the same 6 real photos that caught the original bug, via the new `run_vision_eval.py` harness (`backend/eval/vision_questions.json`): **5/6 passed.** The explicit-brand photo now correctly returns `ambiguous` with all 5 real Azabuya branches (previously: one wrong specific branch, different each retest); the cheesecake and banana-pie photos now correctly return `unknown` instead of a wrong match. The logo-only photo flipped between `unknown` and `ambiguous` across separate runs — both honest outcomes (recognizing the brand from the logo but correctly not guessing a branch is arguably the *more* useful of the two), so the eval case was widened to accept either; what must not happen — a wrong single-branch guess — never did. Also wired the previously-dead-end "unknown" case into a real follow-up: `describe_unmatched_photo` gives a plain description, which the frontend now feeds into a normal chat turn.

   **Chat-layer results from that same run were mixed — and connect back to two different already-known gaps, not one new one:**
   - The **ambiguous-branch** and **Basque-cheesecake** follow-ups scored a clean 2/2 grounded — the agent correctly listed all 5 real Azabuya branches, and correctly recommended `EAU Café`/`bebaked`/`Drunk Baker`'s real 巴斯克 items instead of the old wrong single-store guess.
   - The **banana-pie** and **fictional-store** follow-ups scored 0/2 grounded — but reading the actual replies, both are the *same* already-documented finding #1 gap ("still open," ~1-in-4 rate, no-signal recommendation stretching), just reproducing through this new photo-triggered entry point rather than a new bug: the banana-pie reply disclosed the KB has no exact match, then undercut that by arguing Pie Bird's "product logic naturally extends" to cover it anyway; the fictional-store reply invented three specific fake shop names with addresses and descriptions when asked "somewhere similar." Useful confirmation that finding #1 is real and still open, from an independent angle — not something to re-fix here.
   - The **Bao's Pastry (鲍师傅)** follow-up reproduced its false-brand-equivalence claim a second time, independently, essentially identically (claiming it's "often confused with" `Drunk Baker`/醉师傅, reusing Drunk Baker's real 44-branch count and addresses as if answering the Bao's Pastry question). Two-for-two, not a rare flake — this is a genuinely new, distinct bug from the finding #1 pattern: it doesn't invent a *fake* shop, it falsely equates a real un-cataloged one with a real cataloged one, then answers using the wrong brand's real data.

   ~~**Fixed, mostly verified.**~~ The old verb list in `_FABRICATED_TESTIMONIAL_RE` (说/反馈/提到/...) didn't cover confusion-claim verbs (搞混/认错/confused with) — extended it, and added an explicit rule-2 sentence: naming a real brand doesn't make a reply grounded if it's a *different* real brand than the one asked about. Re-sampled the same Bao's Pastry follow-up 5 times: **4/5 clean** — the agent now explicitly separates the two brands ("鲍师傅和醉师傅是完全不同的品牌"), correctly states the KB doesn't have Bao's Pastry, and in the best samples gives real Bao's Pastry info attributed to a web search instead. One residual sample avoided the specific "customers confuse them" phrase my regex catches, but still recommended Drunk Baker as a stylistic substitute with real addresses, without ever answering about the actual place asked about — confirmed via `_looks_like_unverified_shop_claim` that this softer phrasing slips past every existing check (no price/number attached to the wrong entity, and a real brand name is present, so nothing fires). Genuine improvement (0/2 → 4/5), not a full close — the same "verified but not 100%" honesty as finding #1's still-open status, not chased further given diminishing returns on an increasingly narrow regex.

7. **General practice** — re-run the eval suite as changes land and compare against the saved timestamped results, rather than assuming a fix worked. Every claim in this document was verified this way, not asserted from a single sample. Also found and fixed a separate class of problem this pass: several `kb_boundary`/`unknown_fact`/`adversarial_ambiguous_01` auto-grade failures turned out to be the eval harness's own regex patterns being too literal (missing "没有...记录" phrasing, "doesn't list" in English, a plain "Nope" refusal), not real agent misbehavior — direct inspection of the raw replies showed correct behavior in every one of these cases. Broadened the affected patterns in `test_questions.json`. Worth remembering going forward: a failing auto-grade result needs the same "read the raw reply before trusting the score" discipline as a judge score does, just for a different reason (grader too narrow, vs. grader hallucinating).
